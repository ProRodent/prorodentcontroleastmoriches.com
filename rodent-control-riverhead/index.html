What the calendar does to rodents.

Heavy rain can drive burrowing rats toward drier ground, including the space under a deck or shed. Look for fresh holes and shifted soil once the water recedes.

Edges that rodents use.

Gaps under sheds, decks and porch steps are common bases. Check them first.

Properties with mature trees often have branches over the roof, and a limb that touches shingles works as a footbridge. Trimming it is the cheapest rodent job there is.

Small steps for now.

  • Steer kids and dogs away from the room that shows the most signs.
  • Pull on gloves prior to handling soiled or damp items.
  • Skip the glue boards, which snare small songbirds and lizards too.
  • Snap photos before you clean, since the mess tells us where to look.

Before you call: five habits that keep dust down.

Tempted to tidy before the crew gets there? Take the steps in order, and stop wherever you feel unsure.

  • Nothing dry should be swept, blown or vacuumed, since airborne dust is the main way the germs spread.
  • Before you work in a space that was closed up, open its doors and windows and walk away for about thirty minutes.
  • Skin should not meet pellets, urine spots or shredded nest material, so wear gloves from the first minute.
  • Spray plenty, back away, wait a few minutes, then lift everything with disposable towels.
  • Ordinary garbage service takes the bag, and any reusable tool you used gets rinsed.
  • Illness after contact with rodent waste is a physician's question, so tell the doctor exactly what you touched.
