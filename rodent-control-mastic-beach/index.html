Sand, wrack and salt on a waterside lot.

If your lot is near the water, a few general South Shore points apply. Sandy ground is easy for a rat to dig, storm debris piled against a wall makes cover, and salt air can rust vent screens. Each is quick to check before the visit.

A rusted screen is worth replacing with coated or stainless mesh. A heap of seaweed or driftwood against siding is worth moving. A burrow beside the foundation is worth photographing, with a coin for scale, before you call.

Do this today.

  • Capture burrows, pellets and bedding on camera before any raking.

Before you call: five habits that keep dust down.

Come back to this list whenever pellets, urine stains or torn bedding appear indoors.

  • Nothing dry should be swept, blown or vacuumed, since airborne dust is the main way the germs spread.
  • Before you work in a space that was closed up, open its doors and windows and walk away for about thirty minutes.
  • Skin should not meet pellets, urine spots or shredded nest material, so wear gloves from the first minute.
  • Liquid binds the dust, which is why soaking always precedes wiping.
  • Lift a carcass using a gloved hand or a shovel, with no bare skin involved.
  • Keep tabs on your health across the next few weeks, and see a physician should you develop flu-like illness or labored breathing.
