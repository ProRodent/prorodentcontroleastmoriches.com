Cold weather and rodent timing.

A nor'easter can flood low ground and flush burrows, and it can pile debris against walls. We cannot predict what any storm will do, but a walk around the property afterward is worth the time.

If your lot borders woods or fields.

Branches that touch the roof are footbridges. Trim them back, and clear vines that climb the siding.

Sheds, barns and outbuildings are often the first buildings rodents reach. Check them before you worry about the house.

How the place type affects the job.

Calling a place a hamlet does not make its houses different from a village's. What differs is the lot, the age of the building and the way it is used.

A hamlet is an unincorporated community, which means it has no government of its own and sits inside a town. That has no bearing on rodents, and a little on paperwork.

Interim steps.

  • Latch the door on the loudest room and treat it as off limits.
  • Let pellets lie, because sweeping them launches grit into the air.
  • Pour no bait out on shelves, since a poisoned animal often dies in a place you cannot reach.
  • Scribble the hour each time something thumps overhead.

Before you call: five habits that keep dust down.

Come back to this list whenever pellets, urine stains or torn bedding appear indoors.

  • Leave the broom and the shop vacuum in the closet, because dry droppings crumble and the dust goes straight into your lungs.
  • Create a cross-breeze, wait roughly half an hour, and only then step in without sniffing around.
  • Count every soiled surface as contaminated, and keep gloved fingers away from your eyes and mouth.
  • Soak pellets and urine marks in disinfectant or diluted household bleach and leave the liquid alone for about five minutes.
  • Ordinary garbage service takes the bag, and any reusable tool you used gets rinsed.
  • Our expertise ends at pest work, so a clinician should handle any sickness that follows.
