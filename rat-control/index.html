Where rat trouble begins.

A rat does not wander into a home by chance. It follows food, water and cover, and then it tests every edge until something gives. Usually that begins outside: a feeder that spills seed, a compost bin with a loose lid, a stack of boards lying on the ground.

For that reason, the first visit spends as much time in the yard as in the attic. The technician looks for burrow holes with smooth, packed edges, runs worn into grass, and rub marks on fence rails and sill plates. Those signs tell us whether the animals live under the shed, along the slab or somewhere in the neighbor's hedge.

Sand, salt and storm weather.

South Shore soil is often sandy, and sand is easy to tunnel. It can also slump after heavy rain, which means a burrow beside your foundation may open up, collapse and reopen in a new spot. Salt air can rust ordinary steel mesh, so the choice of coated or stainless material matters when a repair is expected to last through many winters.

Coastal storms and king tides can drive animals off soggy ground toward higher, drier cover. We cannot predict any single storm, and we make no claim that calls rise after one. Still, animals relocate once their ground floods, and whether the garage becomes their new address depends on an unsealed gap.

Reading the yard, then the roofline.

  • Ground level: burrow openings, tracks in loose soil, gnawed bags and chewed pet-food containers.
  • Structures: gaps under sheds, decks, porch steps and the bottom edge of siding.
  • Plumbing and wiring: sleeves where lines enter the foundation or wall.
  • Roofline: soffit panels, ridge and gable vents, plus limbs or cables brushing the shingles.

Findings from this lap shape the plan. An animal that lives outdoors and visits the garage calls for different work than one that has nested in the attic insulation.

Traps, stations and the cost of waiting.

Rats are cautious. They avoid new objects for days, so a trap dropped in the open often sits ignored. Stations are set along walls and burrow mouths and left in place long enough for the animals to accept them. Because of that learning period, a rat job runs on a schedule of revisits, which the technician sets out at the start.

Our trapping page explains station types. If rats have reached the attic or a wall cavity, the attic and crawl space page covers what happens to contaminated insulation.

Habits that shorten a rat job.

Lift pet food bowls at dusk and sweep up seed under feeders every evening. Keep trash cans lidded and set firewood on a rack away from the wall. None of these steps cleans out a nest that already exists, but each one makes the property less rewarding after the work is finished.

Do this today.

  • If leaves or twigs fill the engine bay, keep the car parked pending a check.
  • Move lawn seed, dog chow and feeder mix inside for now.
  • Bag up one chewed item as evidence for whoever visits.
  • Find the breaker panel in case a chewed cable has to be shut off.

Before you call: a safer way to tidy droppings.

The list below paraphrases public health guidance and holds up whether or not you ever book a visit.

  • Leave the broom and the shop vacuum in the closet, because dry droppings crumble and the dust goes straight into your lungs.
  • Airing a closed space for thirty minutes is the CDC's first step before cleanup begins.
  • Skin should not meet pellets, urine spots or shredded nest material, so wear gloves from the first minute.
  • Paper towels, not dry rags, pick up the wet waste, and you keep spraying until nothing looks powdery.
  • Lift a carcass using a gloved hand or a shovel, with no bare skin involved.
  • Our expertise ends at pest work, so a clinician should handle any sickness that follows.

Rat questions from the South Shore.

Norway rat or roof rat?

Norway rats are heavy, short-tailed diggers that stay low. Roof rats are slimmer, long-tailed climbers and are less common in the Northeast. Droppings, burrows and the height of the noise help the technician decide.

Can sandy soil make rat burrows easier?

It may. Loose sand is easy to dig and can also collapse, so a burrow along a foundation often shifts over time. The technician looks for fresh soil, smooth entrances and trails, and not for old holes.

Is scrubbing up after rats overhead risky?

The hazard is airborne grit from dry pellets. Follow the CDC steps in the box here, and hand large messes to a technician.

Will cutting back my shrubs help?

Dense plantings against siding give rats cover for the last few feet of their trip, so trimming shortens that cover. It does not close gaps in a soffit or foundation, so both jobs matter.

Does a storm drive rats indoors?

High water and strong wind can flush burrows and shift where animals shelter. Whether a rat then comes inside depends on whether your building has an opening, which is the part we can fix.

What to have ready for a rat call.

Bird feeders, woodpiles, compost bins, decks and sheds near the house all steer the first walk around. Note which of them you have, and where.
