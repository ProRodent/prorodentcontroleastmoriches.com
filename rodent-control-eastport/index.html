Before you call, hold the broom.

Droppings and nesting scraps can carry germs, so the safe method is slow, wet and unhurried. The CDC pages named at the bottom are the source of each step.

  • Nothing dry should be swept, blown or vacuumed, since airborne dust is the main way the germs spread.
  • Create a cross-breeze, wait roughly half an hour, and only then step in without sniffing around.
  • Skin should not meet pellets, urine spots or shredded nest material, so wear gloves from the first minute.
  • Paper towels, not dry rags, pick up the wet waste, and you keep spraying until nothing looks powdery.
  • Add a second bag when the first is thin or heavy.
  • If something feels off afterward, describe the cleanup to a doctor and let them weigh it.
