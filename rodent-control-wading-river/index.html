What sends people to the phone.

Not every clue means rodents, but these are worth a call.

  • Footprints in a dusty attic.
  • Insulation pulled into piles.
  • Gnawed corners on cardboard in a basement.
  • Droppings along the top of a garage door track.

Do this today.

  • Avoid climbing into the attic or underfloor area before we arrive, because shifted insulation keeps dust floating.
  • A nest is best left untouched, since a startled adult may flee deeper into the walls.
  • Find where the roof access and underfloor door are beforehand so time is not lost.
  • Shift stored boxes off the hatch only when lifting them is easy.

Before you call, what public health agencies advise.

Read every line once before you pick anything up, because a few calm minutes protect your lungs.

  • Using a leaf blower in a garage with droppings is the worst choice, and an ordinary vacuum cleaner comes second.
  • Airing a closed space for thirty minutes is the CDC's first step before cleanup begins.
  • Gloves go on at the start and soap and water come at the end.
  • Treat the surface beneath and around the waste as well, because urine spreads further than the stain shows.
  • Knot the bag before you carry it, and walk it directly to the outdoor trash.
  • If fever, muscle aches or breathing trouble appear within weeks of cleanup, see a physician and mention the rodent waste. We cannot offer medical advice.
