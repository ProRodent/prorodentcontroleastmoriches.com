What rodents actually damage.

Gnawing never stops for rodents, as their incisors keep lengthening. Batting, flex duct, plastic piping, wire sheathing, drywall facing and wood trim all make suitable chewing material. Most damage is quiet for a long time and shows up as a bill later.

The most worrying damage involves wiring. Chewed insulation can expose copper, and exposed copper in an attic or wall is a fire hazard. We never repair wiring ourselves, but we flag it clearly in the estimate.

Why order matters.

Repairs belong at the end. If you replace insulation while animals still have a way in, they chew the new material too. The sequence is trap, seal, clean and then repair, as explained on our sealing and attic cleanup pages.

Moisture and salt on the South Shore.

Coastal houses face humid air, and rodent damage can mix with damp. Wet insulation loses value, ducts can sweat and metal screens can rust. When we replace a vent screen or duct fitting, we choose materials that fit the exposure, such as galvanized or coated metal.

Safety notes.

  • Do not touch a wire that shows bare copper, and shut off power to that circuit if you can do so safely.
  • Wear gloves and a mask when handling old insulation.
  • Keep children and pets away from damaged areas.
  • Photograph everything before anything is moved.

Before you call: how the CDC says to clean up.

These federal pages are summarized in loose terms. For health questions, a doctor is the better source than a pest company.

  • Leave the broom and the shop vacuum in the closet, because dry droppings crumble and the dust goes straight into your lungs.
  • Open every opening in the room, then do something else for thirty minutes before you return.
  • Thin disposable gloves handle small messes, and heavier rubber pairs handle bigger ones and can be rinsed afterward.
  • Spray plenty, back away, wait a few minutes, then lift everything with disposable towels.
  • Drop soiled towels and any dead animal into one plastic bag, using gloves or tongs, then tie it and place it in the household trash.
  • Illness after contact with rodent waste is a physician's question, so tell the doctor exactly what you touched.

Repair questions.

Do you repair electrical wiring?

No. We note chewed wiring and advise you to hire an electrician. Stay away from exposed copper.

Why wait to repair?

Repairs made before sealing can be chewed again. Closing the entries first protects the new work.

Is insulation always replaced?

Only when it is matted, soiled or soaked with urine. Clean, dry sections may stay.

Might my homeowner policy pay for this?

Coverage differs between plans, and some exclude rodents entirely. Check with your insurer and hold on to the photos and estimate we supply.

Can small holes in drywall be patched?

Yes. Small holes are usually cut back to sound material, patched and finished. Larger areas may need new sheets.

What a repair visit includes.

Houses of every age can need repairs. Take pictures of tooth-marked insulation, flex duct and cable jackets, and stay away from any wire whose copper shows.
